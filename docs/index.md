# PIFRA Registration Documentation: Every Form Field Explained

This documentation explains, field by field, the online registration form used by PIFRA — the Project to Improve Financial Reporting and Auditing. PIFRA works under the FABS and AGPR framework and is the system through which federal and provincial government employees in Pakistan receive their monthly salary slips by email instead of collecting a printed slip from the accounts office. Registration is a one-time process carried out on the official portal, m.pifra.gov.pk. Once your registration is accepted and your email address is verified, your pay slip is delivered to your inbox every month. This page walks through each field, what it means, what to enter, and the mistakes that most often cause rejection.

## Before You Begin

Keep the following documents and details within reach before you open the form. Entering data from memory is the most common cause of a failed registration, because the form validates your entries against the payroll records held by your accounts office. You will need your personnel number, your CNIC, your date of birth as recorded in your service record, your DDO code, an active mobile number, and an email address that you control and check regularly.

## Field 1: Personnel Number

The personnel number is the unique identification number assigned to you by your accounts office. It appears on your existing salary slip and in your service record. Enter it exactly as printed, including any leading zeros, and do not add spaces or dashes unless the form separates the number into boxes for you. The most frequent error in this field is entering the CNIC number instead of the personnel number. The two are different identifiers, and the system checks the personnel number against the payroll record, so a wrong entry here stops the registration immediately.

## Field 2: CNIC

Your CNIC is your thirteen-digit Computerised National Identity Card number issued by NADRA. Type it carefully in the format the form requests. The CNIC you enter must be the one recorded against your personnel number in the accounts office data. If your CNIC was ever renewed, reissued, or corrected, use the number that your department and accounts office hold on file, because that is the record the registration is matched against. A single transposed digit in this field is one of the leading causes of rejection.

## Field 3: Date of Birth

Enter the date of birth exactly as it appears in your official service record. This sounds obvious, but many employees discover that the date held by the accounts office differs from the date they personally use, sometimes because of an old clerical entry made at the time of appointment. The form validates against the official record, not against your memory. If your entries keep failing, compare your CNIC and service book first, and have any genuine discrepancy corrected through your office and accounts office rather than trying different dates at random.

## Field 4: DDO Code

The DDO code identifies your Drawing and Disbursing Officer — the officer through whom your salary is drawn. Through this code, the system attaches your registration to the correct office and the correct accounts circle. You can find your DDO code printed on your salary slip; if it is not there, your office establishment section or your accounts office can provide it. Entering the code of a previous office after a transfer is a common mistake, and it can attach your registration to the wrong disbursing office.

## Field 5: Mobile Number

Enter an active mobile number, ideally one registered in your own name and one you expect to keep for a long time. The number is used for verification during registration and for account communication. Do not use a colleague's number, a number belonging to a family member that you cannot access, or a temporary SIM you plan to discard, because losing access to this number later makes account recovery unnecessarily difficult.

## Field 6: Email Address

Treat this as the most important field on the form. Your monthly salary slip is delivered to this email address, so a typing mistake here means your slips simply never arrive. Use a personal email account that only you control — not a shared office inbox and not an account created for you by someone else. Type the address slowly, check it character by character before submitting, and make sure you know the password for the account so you can open the verification message and, later, your slips.

## Common Mistakes That Cause Rejection

Most failed registrations come from a small set of repeat errors: a CNIC or date of birth that does not match the accounts office record; a DDO code from a previous posting; a misspelled email address; an incorrect personnel number; and registering a second time with a different email address instead of fixing the first registration, which creates confusion about which account is active. Every one of these is avoidable by preparing your documents in advance and reviewing each field before you submit.

## What Happens After Submission

After you submit the form, your details are verified against the payroll data held for your personnel number. When verification succeeds and your email address is confirmed, your registration becomes active and your salary slip begins arriving by email in the following pay cycles. If your first slip does not arrive, check your spam or junk folder before assuming the registration failed. When you are transferred and your DDO changes, or when your recorded particulars are corrected by your office, the updated data flows from your accounts office — core payroll information cannot be edited through the registration form itself.
